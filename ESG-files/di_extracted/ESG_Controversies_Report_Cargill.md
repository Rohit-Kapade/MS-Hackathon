Cargill, Incorporated (Cargill) has faced criticism from environmental protection groups for trading and processing soy, allegedly linked to deforestation in
protected areas of Brazil and Bolivia. The company has also faced a legal complaint in the U.S. for its alleged failure to eliminate deforestation from its supply
chain.

LATEST CASE DEVELOPMENTS

In February 2025, Reporter Brasil criticized Cargill for allegedly failing to follow the rules established under the Soy Moratorium, which the company had
previously committed to. Instead of banning trade in soy grown on lands deforested in the Amazon biome after 2008, the company reportedly shifted the
cutoff year to 2020, which was the limit set by the European Union Deforestation Regulation. The article alleged that using the 2008 benchmark, Cargill
claimed that 94% of its soy was deforestation-free, while under the 2020 benchmark, this figure rose to 99.3%.

BACKGROUND (FROM LEAST TO MOST RECENT)

In February 2017, Mighty Earth published a report entitled "The Ultimate Mystery Meat," wherein it highlighted the alleged role of several companies in
the deforestation of forests in Brazil and Bolivia due to their sourcing of soy. The report criticized Cargill for financing infrastructure projects that allegedly
contributed to deforestation and for setting weak deforestation targets. Additionally, Mighty Earth claimed that the area where Cargill operated a commercial
silo in Brazil's Cerrado region appeared to have experienced 1,300 square kilometers of deforestation between 2011 and 2015.

In January 2018, Mighty Earth announced that 61 companies had signed the Cerrado Manifesto, a pledge to eliminate deforestation for large-scale agriculture
in the Cerrado. The manifesto called on Cargill to extend its deforestation-free commitments from the Amazon to the Cerrado and other areas in Latin
America.

In May 2018, several grain traders, including Cargill, were fined a total of BRL 105.7 million (approximately USD 28.8 million) by IBAMA. The agency stated
that the penalty was due to the companies' connection to alleged illegal deforestation in the country.

In August 2018, Mongabay alleged that soy producers in Brazil's Cerrado and Bolivia's Gran Chaco continued to deforest the regions. The NGO further claimed
that the soy grown in these areas was purchased by traders such as Cargill.

In June 2019, Mighty Earth published a report on deforestation caused by soy and beef production. The report alleged that Cargill was one of the main soy
buyers in four locations within the Amazon and the Cerrado.

In July 2019, The New York Times reported that Mighty Earth released a report entitled "Cargill: The Worst Company in the World." The report criticized
Cargill for allegedly failing to meet anti-deforestation goals related to its sourcing of soy and for refusing to implement a moratorium on purchasing soy grown
on deforested lands in Brazil's Cerrado region.

In November 2020, The Guardian indicated that a joint report it prepared along with other agencies revealed that Cargill had allegedly been linked to the
destruction of forests in the Cerrado region through its sourcing of soy. The commodity is then used to feed chicken supplied to supermarkets and fast food
chains in the U.K. The report further alleged that Cargill failed to take sufficient measures to prevent deforestation in its supply chain.

In January 2022, The Guardian alleged that Cargill, a feed supplier to farm animals in the U.K., continued to source soy from a farm allegedly linked to
deforestation in the Brazilian Amazon. These findings reportedly contrasted Cargill's commitment to not purchase soy from deforested lands in the Amazon
after 2008.

In July 2022, Mighty Earth published a report on its investigation into the deforestation of Brazil's Cerrado and its links to soy cultivation and purchasing by
traders. In particular, Cargill was linked to eight farms that were allegedly responsible for 247 square kilometers of illegal deforestation since August 2020.

In May 2023, the environmental law organization ClientEarth filed a complaint against Cargill over allegations that the company failed to conduct due
diligence in its soy supply chain.

In September 2023, Global Witness published the findings of its investigation into soy grown in Bolivia. The NGO alleged that Cargill failed to trace the origins
of its soy supplies in Bolivia, which meant that the company could not guarantee that its purchases were deforestation-free. The report further estimated
that the soy the company purchased may have come from farms linked to 200 square kilometers of deforestation in Bolivia's Chiquitano forest.

In November 2023, Mongabay reported that Cargill announced that it had committed to having its supply chains in Brazil, Uruguay, and Argentina be
deforestation- and land-conversion-free by 2025. In response, Mighty Earth criticized the company for its alleged failure to include other countries such as
Bolivia, Paraguay, and Colombia in the commitment.

In March 2024, Mighty Earth published its second rapid response report on deforestation caused by soy cultivation in Brazil. The report connected Cargill to
two suppliers that were allegedly linked to deforestation in both the Amazon and the Cerrado regions. Specifically, the NGO claimed that one Cargill supplier
had received several environmental fines from Brazil's environmental agency, IBAMA, for alleged illegal clearing of native vegetation. According to the report,
the supplier was allegedly responsible for 21.3 square kilometers of deforestation in the Amazon between January 2020 and December 2023. In the same
month, Reporter Brasil alleged that deforestation persisted in Cargill's supply chain despite the company's no-deforestation commitments.

In October 2024, Mighty Earth published a report in partnership with AGtivist. The report alleged a link between Cargill and the illegal deforestation of 64.41
square kilometers of Brazil's Cerrado. Cargill, in turn, reportedly exported soy to a poultry meat producer that supplied supermarkets and fast food chains.

In December 2024, Mighty Earth alleged that Cargill was one of two soy traders in the U.S. linked to the bulk of deforestation in Brazilian biomes. The NGO
specifically alleged that Cargill was "likely linked" to over 1,332.56 square kilometers of deforestation, of which 85% occurred in the Cerrado region.

#### BACKGROUND (FROM LEAST TO MOST RECENT)

In June 2021, Forest Peoples Programme published a press release criticizing Cargill and other companies for their alleged links to human rights abuses

defending their lands.

In March 2022, FoE published a report entitled "No Consent: Astra Agro Lestari's land grab in Central and West Sulawesi, Indonesia," which alleged that

taken.

More specifically, FoE alleged that in 2004, PT Lestari Tana Teladan (PT LTT) and a unit of the Indonesian National Police forcefully seized land from villages

were criminalized and imprisoned for several months for protesting the takeover. PT Mamuang was also reportedly involved in multiple conflicts with
residents in farming communities. In one instance in 2017, four farmers who were harvesting palm oil on land for which they held legal ownership permits
were allegedly criminalized and arrested for several months. Meanwhile, another farmer was reportedly subject to "violence, imprisonment, and threats to
his life and his family" after attempting to resist the forceful takeover of his land by PT Mamuang. Palm oil from AAL and its subsidiaries reportedly flowed to
Cargill.

their lands, which adversely affected their access to livelihoods and food. In addition, The Guardian reported that a railroad backed by Cargill to transport
soya grown in the Cerrado could harm six Indigenous territories and three tribes.

In March 2024, Reporter Brasil published a report alleging that Cargill and other traders purchased soy from farmers who had previously been fined for
planting on the Indigenous land of the Paresi people in Mato Grosso. Moreover, Mundukuru representatives alleged that Cargill's infrastructure projects in

In September 2021, the Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Crisis, a subcommittee established by the United States House of Representatives,
announced that it expanded its investigation into COVID-19 outbreaks at meatpacking facilities to include Cargill. The inquiries followed allegations that
COVID-19 had severely impacted workers in meatpacking plants, leading to illnesses and deaths. According to the subcommittee, 1,394 Cargill employees
were allegedly infected by the virus. In addition, one Cargill plant was allegedly linked to the spread of COVID-19 in the community.

In October 2021, Reuters reported that the Select Subcommittee on the Coronavirus Crisis revealed that 59,147 workers in meatpacking facilities had been
infected with COVID-19, and 269 workers had died. The subcommittee further noted that this number was far higher than the one previously disclosed by
the Occupational Safety and Health Administration.

In June 2024, an arbitrator ruled that Cargill does not need to pay damages for its handling of the COVID-19 outbreak in its High River meatpacking plant
in Alberta, Canada. This arbitration ruling was a product of a grievance complaint filed by the United Food and Commercial Workers, Local No. 401 against
Cargill in April 2020. The grievance broadly alleged that the company failed to protect its workers against COVID-19 and did not involve the union in
COVID-19-related safety measures.

### POTENTIAL STATUS UPDATE CONDITIONS FOR ESG CONTROVERSY CASES:

MSCI ESG Research does not provide recommendations regarding actions to be taken in response to allegations. However, the following
conditions may be considered during the review of a controversy case:

· Dismissal of Allegations: A case may be assessed as concluded or partially concluded if allegations are formally dismissed or refuted
by a court, an external investigation, or an independent audit.

· Revocation of Allegations: A case may be reassessed if the party or parties raising the allegations withdraw their claims.

· Remedial Action or Resolution: A case may be reviewed for status updates where the company has taken steps to address the issues
raised-such as reaching agreements with affected stakeholders, implementing remediation measures, or ceasing the relevant
practices or engagements.

. Lack of Sustained Attention: A lack of ongoing negative publicity, stakeholder criticism, or new developments over an extended period
(typically 2-3 years) may be a condition for status reassessment.

Note: A controversy case generally remains open if criticism or credible concerns persist, even if the company has responded or made
remediation efforts.
